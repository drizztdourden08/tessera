`ControllerLayout` draws a plain outline of a controller with every input in its place, like a console test screen. `variant` picks the body: `offset` for Xbox shapes, `symmetric` for PlayStation shapes with the sticks side by side, `nintendo` for the offset body with face labels from the caller, and `pad` for a pad with no sticks or triggers. `slots` takes a state per input by its place, such as `face.south`, `dpad.up`, `trigger.left` or `stick.right.click`; `CONTROLLER_SLOT_IDS` lists them and `ControllerSlotId` is their union. A held button lights in the primary colour, triggers and shoulders fill with `value` from 0 to 1, a stick cap moves with `x` and `y` from -1 to 1 with `y` up, and a stick click lights the cap. A `label` or a `glyph` shows on or beside its control. A slot the variant has no place for, and every entry of `extras`, shows in a list under the outline, one row each. The outline grows with its card up to `--controller-layout-max-w` (640 px). Screen readers hear the figure name and a hidden list of what is held now, so a moving stick announces nothing. A new strings group, `controller`, holds the names of every input.
